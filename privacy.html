Privacy policy for americanbasilicas.org

Last updated: November 5, 2026

American Basilicas runs americanbasilicas.org: the map of the U.S. minor basilicas and the free member accounts that go with it (My pilgrimage, the members’ gallery and each basilica’s members’ page). This policy explains what this website collects, why, who can see it, and how to delete it.

Shopping is covered by the store’s policy. Our online store runs on Shopify and has its own privacy policy. It covers browsing the store, orders, payment, shipping, the store’s cookies and marketing from the store. For anything to do with the store, that policy is the one that applies.

Who we are and how to contact us

This website is run by American Basilicas, which is the data controller for the information described here. Questions about this policy, or requests about your information, can go to americanbasilicas@gmail.com. Our other contact details are on the store’s contact information page.

What we collect and why

If you just use the map

You don’t need an account to use the map. This website doesn’t set its own cookies, and it has no analytics or advertising trackers.

  • Hosting. The website is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, it receives your IP address and basic browser information when your browser loads a page.
  • Pictures from other sites. Some pictures load straight from other services, which then also receive your IP address: product pictures come from Shopify, and for basilicas without a photo of our own, the map may load one from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons.
  • Directions. “Directions” links open Google Maps only when you tap them.
  • Basilicas near you. On My pilgrimage you can ask for the basilicas nearest you. Your browser first asks your permission to share your location. The distances are worked out on your device; your location isn’t sent to us or saved. If you’d rather not share your location, you can type a ZIP code instead. It’s looked up on your device from a list that comes with the site (ZIP code centers from the U.S. Census Bureau), and it isn’t sent anywhere or saved.

If you create a member account

  • Your email address, to sign you in. You can sign in with a link or 6-digit code that we email you, or with “Continue with Google”. Your email address is never shown to other members.
  • If you use Google: Google shares your name, email address and profile picture with our sign-in service, which keeps them with your login. We use the first word of your Google name as your starting display name. We don’t show your Google profile picture on the site.
  • Sign-in records: our sign-in service records when you sign in and, for security, technical details of sign-in requests such as IP address and browser.
  • Display name. It starts as your Google first name or “Pilgrim”, and you can change it at any time under My pilgrimage → Account.
  • Your visits: which basilicas you’ve checked off, the dates you enter and your private notes.
  • Your photos and captions. Before a photo leaves your device, your browser resizes it (to at most 2,000 pixels, plus a 480-pixel copy) and saves it as a new JPEG. This removes the hidden details photos usually carry, such as GPS location and camera information. Photos are kept in private storage.
  • What you post: reviews, questions and answers, and which photos and reviews you’ve liked.
  • Moderation details: whether each item is waiting for review, shared or not shared, and when it was reviewed.
  • Gallery access: when you created your account (the start of the three free months) and whether you’ve seen the welcome message.
  • Membership. If you subscribe through Buy Me a Coffee, Buy Me a Coffee tells us your email address, membership level, status and the date you’ve paid through. We match this to your account by email address. We never receive your card or payment details.
  • Order history. When you open Orders on My pilgrimage, the website asks our Shopify store for orders placed with your verified email address and shows them to you. We don’t copy your orders into the member database.

We use this information only to run the map and member features: signing you in, keeping your pilgrimage, sharing what you choose to post once it has been reviewed, opening the gallery to members and subscribers, and keeping the site safe, for example with daily posting limits. The website sends you only sign-in emails. We don’t sell the information described here, and we don’t use it for advertising.

What other members can see

  • Only you: your email address, your visits, dates and notes, your likes, your membership details, and any photo, review, question or answer that hasn’t been approved.
  • Other signed-in members: your display name.
  • Members with gallery access: photos (with captions), reviews, questions and answers once they’ve been approved, together with your display name, like counts and a “Has visited” mark if you had checked off that basilica when you posted. Every photo you add to a visit is reviewed for the gallery. Shared photos are shown through links that expire.
  • American Basilicas reviews photos, reviews, questions and answers before they’re shared, and can see membership records to manage gallery access.

Member content isn’t public: the gallery and members’ pages need a signed-in account with gallery access, and they’re marked not to appear in search engines.

Services that help run the site

These companies process information for us so the website can work. Each handles it under its own privacy terms. Your information is stored and processed mainly in the United States.

  • GitHub Pages hosts the website.
  • Supabase runs sign-in, the member database and photo storage, on servers in the eastern United States (Ohio).
  • Resend sends the sign-in emails.
  • Google handles “Continue with Google”, only if you choose it. The “Continue with Google” button on the sign-in form comes from Google, so when that form is on screen your browser loads it from Google, and Google may receive that page visit and set its own cookies, under Google’s privacy policy.
  • Shopify runs our store and answers the order-history lookup. Product pictures on the map also load from Shopify.
  • Buy Me a Coffee handles memberships and their payments.
  • Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons supply some basilica pictures.

Cookies and browser storage

This website doesn’t use cookies for tracking or advertising. When you sign in, the sign-in service saves your session in your browser’s storage so you stay signed in. Signing out removes it. Google’s sign-in button, which loads only while a sign-in form is on screen, may set Google’s own cookies (see “Services that help run the site” above).

Because this website doesn’t sell or share your information for advertising, there’s nothing to opt out of here. The store’s cookies and its advertising choices are described in the store’s privacy policy, and you can use the store’s data sharing opt-out page.

How long we keep things, and deleting your account

We keep your account information for as long as you have an account, so your pilgrimage is there when you come back.

  • Delete one thing at a time. You can delete a photo, review, question or answer, or remove a visit (which also deletes that visit’s photos), whenever you like.
  • Delete everything. My pilgrimage → Account → Delete my account permanently deletes your login, display name, visits and notes, photos and their files, reviews, questions, answers and likes. Answers other members gave to your questions are removed with them.
  • Membership records. A Buy Me a Coffee membership record is kept by email address, separately from your account, so a membership you’ve paid for still works if you sign up again. Email us and we’ll delete it.
  • Store orders. Deleting your account here doesn’t affect your store orders; those are covered by the store’s privacy policy.
  • Service provider records. Our service providers may keep limited records, such as sign-in logs or a record of emails sent, for a period set by their own policies.

Your choices and rights

On the website you can change your display name, edit or delete what you’ve posted, and delete your account yourself.

Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to ask us for access to the personal information we hold about you, to correct or delete it, or to receive a copy of it. If you live in the UK or European Economic Area, you may also be able to object to or restrict our processing, or withdraw consent you’ve given. These rights aren’t absolute and may apply only in some circumstances.

  • To make a request, email americanbasilicas@gmail.com. We may need to verify your identity first. Someone you authorize can make a request for you, with proof that you’ve authorized them.
  • We won’t discriminate against you for exercising these rights, and we’ll respond in a timely manner as required by applicable law.
  • If you have a complaint, contact us first. Depending on where you live, you may be able to appeal our decision or complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

The website’s member accounts aren’t intended for children, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from children under the age of majority where they live. If you’re a parent or guardian and believe your child has given us personal information, contact us and we’ll delete it.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the website handles your information, we’ll post the new policy on this page, update the “Last updated” date above, and give any notice required by applicable law.
